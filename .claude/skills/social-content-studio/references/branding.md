## 1. What a social brand is

On social, a brand is what people expect when they see your name in the feed: the topics, the attitude, the look, and the value they'll get. A strong brand is recognisable before the viewer reads the handle, and it has a clear answer to "why should I follow this?"

That expectation is built by repetition: the same promise, voice and look, kept up post after post.

## 2. Brand foundation

Write these down, in `content/brand-brief.md` via `assets/brand-brief-template.md`:

**Positioning statement**
> For [specific audience] who [problem/desire], [brand] is the [category/role] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative], because [reason to believe].

**Audience (one primary persona)**
- Who they are: role, life stage, situation. Be specific: "first-time managers at startups", not "professionals".
- What they struggle with, in **their own words**. Mine comments, reviews, Reddit and sales calls, and reuse the phrases as hooks.
- What they want (the outcome) and what they've already tried.
- What they watch and follow, and when and where they scroll.

**Content promise ("Follow for…")**
One line that says what a follower gets: "Follow for 30-second recipes under $10", "Honest reviews from a nurse", "Startup lessons I learned the expensive way". It goes in the bio, and every post should keep it.

**Point of view**
What does the brand believe that others in the niche don't? What does it push back against (bad advice, a status quo, an industry habit)? A clear point of view creates fans and comments. Neutral brands are forgettable.

**Content pillars**
3–5 recurring themes that support the promise (see `strategy.md`). Example for a budget fitness brand: (1) 10-minute home workouts, (2) cheap high-protein meals, (3) myth-busting fitness marketing, (4) member transformations, (5) behind the scenes of the gym.

## 3. Voice and personality

- **Three adjectives**, each with a "but not": "Confident *but not* arrogant. Funny *but not* silly. Expert *but not* jargon-heavy."
- **Words we use / words we never use.** Example: we say "you" and "let's" and plain verbs; we never say "synergy", "game-changer" or "unlock your potential".
- **Sentence style:** short and punchy vs. warm and flowing; emoji use (none / sparing / signature emoji); slang level; humour style.
- **CTA style:** "Save this for later" vs. "Tag your gym buddy" vs. "Thoughts?"
- **Before/after examples:** rewrite one generic sentence in the brand voice. Generic: "Our product helps you save time." On-brand: "Give us 4 minutes. We'll give you your Sunday back."

When writing for a brand, re-read its voice section and then do a voice pass on the finished script. Generic marketing tone is the most common failure.

## 4. Visual identity

- **Colors:** 1–2 brand colors + 1 accent + neutrals. Use them in text overlays, carousel templates, covers, wardrobe and props. Consistency matters more than any particular choice.
- **Typography:** one bold display font for hooks/titles and one clean sans-serif for body/captions. Check that they're legible on a phone at a glance.
- **Caption style:** the same burned-in caption look on every video (font, size, outline, highlight color) is a recognisable signature.
- **Look and grade:** a consistent color temperature and contrast, a recurring set or backdrop, a recurring framing.
- **Templates:** carousel (cover, inner slides, CTA slide), Reel cover, quote card, Story frames, highlight covers. Templates make consistency the easy default.
- **Logo/avatar:** for a personal brand, use a clear, well-lit face, which usually outperforms a logo. For a company, use a simple logo mark readable at 110px. Use the same avatar on every platform.
- **Grid:** since 2025 the Instagram grid uses 3:4 tiles. Plan covers in 3:4 so the grid looks intentional. Don't sacrifice content for a grid aesthetic, because most people see posts in the feed, not on the profile.

## 5. Profile optimization

The profile converts visitors into followers. Treat it as a landing page: within about 5 seconds a visitor should know who this is for, what they'll get, and why to trust it.

### Instagram
| Element | Best practice |
|---|---|
| **Username** | Short, memorable, same across platforms; avoid numbers/underscores if possible |
| **Name field** | Searchable, so include a keyword: "Maya · Budget Meal Prep" rather than just "Maya" |
| **Photo** | Face (personal brand) or simple logo (company), high contrast, centered |
| **Bio (150 chars)** | Who you help + outcome / promise + proof + CTA. Example: "Meal prep for busy nurses 🍱 / $40 → 5 days of food / 120k fed / ↓ free plan" |
| **Link** | One link-in-bio page with the top 3–5 destinations, ordered by goal |
| **Highlights** | 4–6 with branded covers: Start Here, Results/Reviews, Products/Services, FAQ, Behind the Scenes |
| **Pinned posts (3)** | (1) Who I am / the brand story, (2) the best-performing post, (3) the offer or lead magnet |
| **Category / contact** | Set the category; add contact and action buttons for businesses |

### Other platforms
- **TikTok:** bio ≤80 chars with a clear "follow for" line, a link once eligible, and playlists for series.
- **YouTube:** a channel name and handle that match; a banner with the promise and upload schedule; a trailer; a Shorts → long-form playlist strategy.
- **LinkedIn:** a headline that states who you help and how (not just a job title), a banner with the promise, the Featured section with the top post/lead magnet, and a creator-mode style "talks about" topic list.

## 6. Personal brand vs. business brand vs. founder-led

| | Personal brand | Business brand | Founder-led hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face | The creator | Product, team, customers | Founder + team + product |
| Strength | Trust, connection, reach | Scale, longevity, sellable | Trust + business outcomes |
| Risk | Depends on one person | Can feel faceless and ad-like | Founder time |
| Works best on | All platforms | Instagram, TikTok with UGC creators | LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram |

Founder-led and lo-fi, human content outperforms polished brand-voice content in many 2026 analyses. Even business brands should put real people on camera.

## 7. Signature elements and recurring series

Small repeated elements make a brand recognisable in half a second:
- A catchphrase, sign-on or sign-off ("Let's get into it" is generic; something specific to the brand is better).
- A recurring opening shot, set or prop.
- A caption/text style and a color accent.
- Named series ("Budget Swap Sundays", "Résumé Roast #14").
- A recurring character or format (the skeptical friend, the client call re-enactment).

## 8. Trust and community

- **Proof:** results, numbers, testimonials, UGC, press, credentials. Show them rather than claiming them.
- **Behind the scenes:** process, team, mistakes, decisions. Transparency builds trust faster than polish.
- **Reply to comments**, especially in the first hour. Reply with videos to good questions.
- **DMs and Stories** for warm relationships: polls, Q&A stickers, "reply with X and I'll send it".
- **Feature the community:** reshare customer content (with permission and credit), shout-outs, collabs.
- **Consistency of behaviour:** how the brand responds to criticism is part of the brand.

## 9. Consistency without boredom

Keep these fixed: the promise, pillars, voice, visual system and series.
Change these: hooks, formats, angles, guests, locations, and the trends you adapt.
Audiences follow for the consistency and keep watching for the variety.

## 10. Brand audit checklist

- [ ] A stranger can say who this is for and what they'll get within 5 seconds of opening the profile
- [ ] The name field and bio contain searchable keywords
- [ ] The last 9 posts are recognisably from the same brand (look, voice, topics)
- [ ] Every recent post maps to a pillar
- [ ] There's at least one recurring series
- [ ] Highlights cover: start here, proof, offer, FAQ
- [ ] Pinned posts are current
- [ ] The CTA path works: post → profile → link → offer
- [ ] The same handle and avatar are used across platforms
- [ ] The brand voice is documented and actually used in captions
