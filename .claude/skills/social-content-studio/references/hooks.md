## 1. Anatomy of a hook

A viewer decides to stay or swipe in under 3 seconds, often before hearing a word. A hook works when three layers say the same thing at once:

| Layer | Job | Guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** (first frame + motion) | Stop the thumb | Start mid-action. Movement toward the camera, a strange object, the result shown first, a face with a strong expression, hands doing something. Never a logo, black frame or slow fade. |
| **Text** (on-screen) | Explain it on mute; give the platform a keyword | 3–7 words, large, high contrast, in the upper-middle safe zone, on screen from frame 1 |
| **Spoken** (first line) | Open a loop | About 10 words or fewer, so it's done within ~3s. Make it the most interesting sentence in the video. |

The text and spoken hooks don't have to be identical. Often the text names the topic ("Sourdough mistake #1") while the voice opens the loop ("This is why your bread is flat.").

**Payoff contract.** Every hook makes a promise. If the video doesn't deliver it, viewers swipe at the moment they realise, retention falls off a cliff, and they learn not to trust your hooks. The best hooks are bold *and* fully paid off.

## 2. The 16 hook types

Pick from **different** types when generating candidates. Five variations of one type is really one option.

1. **Call-out.** Name the exact viewer.
   "If you're a nurse working nights, this is for you." · "Freelancers who hate invoicing, watch this."
2. **Mistake / negative.** People are wired to avoid losses.
   "You're storing your coffee wrong." · "Stop posting at 9 a.m. if you want views."
3. **Curiosity gap.** Imply missing information.
   "Nobody tells you this about buying a used car." · "There's a setting on your iPhone that's draining your battery."
4. **Result first / proof.** Show the outcome, then explain.
   "This $12 lamp made my apartment look like a hotel." · "We went from 0 to 400 orders with this one email."
5. **Contrarian / hot take.** Challenge a belief the audience holds.
   "Unpopular opinion: you don't need a morning routine." · "Cold calling isn't dead. You're just doing it wrong."
6. **Number / list.** Sets expectations and signals progress.
   "3 things I'd never buy as a dentist." · "5 Canva tricks that look like you paid a designer."
7. **Story in medias res.** Start at the most tense moment.
   "I was 20 minutes from losing my biggest client." · "The inspector opened the oven and went silent."
8. **Question.** Only one the viewer *can't* answer with "no" and leave.
   Bad: "Do you want to save money?" Good: "Why does every hotel use white sheets?"
9. **Comparison / versus.**
   "$5 vs $500 sunscreen, tested." · "What a junior vs senior designer does with the same brief."
10. **Challenge / experiment.**
   "I only ate food from gas stations for 7 days." · "I asked 50 founders the same question."
11. **POV / relatable.** An identity or situation the viewer recognises.
   "POV: your toddler found the permanent marker." · "Every small business owner on the 1st of the month:"
12. **Direct how-to.** Plain value, best for search.
   "How to remove a stripped screw in 10 seconds." · "How to write a cold email that gets replies."
13. **Authority / credibility.** Earned perspective.
   "I've reviewed 3,000 résumés. Here's the #1 reason I reject them." · "As a vet, I'd never feed my dog this."
14. **Warning / urgency.**
   "Don't sign a lease until you check these 3 things." · "Delete this app before you travel."
15. **Secret / behind the curtain.**
   "What restaurants don't want you to know about the specials." · "Here's how much I actually made from 1M views."
16. **Transformation tease.** Before → (cut) → after.
   "This room cost $300 to fix. Wait for it." · Text: "Day 1 vs Day 90".

## 3. Fill-in-the-blank hook bank

Swap in specifics. A specific noun is the difference between a template and a hook.

- "Stop [common action] if you want [desired result]."
- "You're [doing X] wrong. Here's the fix."
- "[Number] [things] I wish I knew before [milestone]."
- "Nobody talks about this part of [topic]."
- "This is the [cheapest / easiest / fastest] way to [result]."
- "I tried [popular thing] for [time] so you don't have to."
- "The [topic] mistake that cost me [specific loss]."
- "[Specific person] asked me [question]. Here's my honest answer."
- "If I had to start [X] from zero in [year], I'd do this."
- "Here's why your [thing] keeps [frustrating outcome]."
- "Steal my [template / routine / script] for [result]."
- "[Number]% of [group] get this wrong."
- "What [$ amount] gets you in [place/category]."
- "The difference between [amateur] and [pro] [thing]."
- "Don't buy [product] until you see this."
- "I was today years old when I learned [fact]."
- "Things in [niche] that just make sense:"
- "Rating [things] as a [credential]."
- "[Thing] in 30 seconds. Go."
- "The one [tool / habit / question] that changed [outcome] for me."
- "This [everyday object] can [unexpected use]."
- "You need to stop saying [phrase] to [person]."
- "Here's exactly what I'd do with [budget / time]."
- "Reply to @[user]: [their question as on-screen text]."
- "Watch this before your next [event]."
- "Why [surprising fact] is actually true."
- "Here's how [famous brand/person] [did impressive thing], and how you can copy it."
- "It took me [long time] to learn this. You'll get it in [short time]."
- "The [niche] tier list nobody asked for."
- "Things I'd never do as a [credential]."

## 4. Visual hooks (no words needed)

These are strongest when combined with a text hook:
- **Result first:** the finished dish, the clean room, the payout screen, then "here's how".
- **Before/after split** or a quick-cut transformation.
- **Unexpected object or action:** a pineapple on a laptop, someone hammering a phone.
- **Motion into the frame:** lean in toward the lens, a product thrown into frame, a door opening.
- **Process mid-action:** pouring, cutting, peeling, a satisfying close-up.
- **Extreme close-up → pull back** to reveal the context.
- **Strong face:** a real reaction (shock, laughter, disgust) in the first frame.
- **Text-over-action:** a bold question over B-roll that's already moving.
- **Pattern break:** an unusual location, costume, camera angle or prop for the niche.

## 5. Re-hooks: keeping people past the middle

Retention graphs usually sag in the middle. Put a re-hook every ~5–8 seconds:
- **Progress markers:** "Number two is the one most people miss."
- **Turns:** "But here's the problem." / "That's what I thought too, until…"
- **Stakes:** "And this is the part that actually costs you money."
- **Payoff tease:** "The last one is my favourite." Only use it if the last one *is* the best.
- **Visual resets:** angle change, punch-in, B-roll, a graphic, a prop, or a location change.
- **Audio resets:** an SFX hit, a music change, or a beat of silence before a reveal.

Avoid stock filler re-hooks like "but wait, there's more" and "keep watching till the end". Viewers recognise them as stalling. Specific re-hooks beat generic ones.

## 6. Hook rules

- **Lead with the payoff's most interesting part**, not the background.
- **No throat-clearing:** cut "Hey guys", "So today I want to talk about", "In this video", intros, name/brand introductions and logo stings.
- **Specific beats general:** "3 a.m. feeds" beats "parenting is hard"; "$47" beats "cheap".
- **Use "you" or a named identity** so the viewer feels addressed.
- **One idea.** A hook that needs a comma probably holds two ideas.
- **Match the platform:** TikTok tolerates rawer, more personal hooks; LinkedIn wants a clear professional promise; Shorts benefits from searchable phrasing.
- **Say the keyword early** (spoken + text) so platforms classify the video correctly.
- **Don't bait-and-switch.** Bold is fine, false is not.

### Hook strength check (score each 0–2; aim for 8+/10)
1. Would a stranger know within 2 seconds who this is for?
2. Is there an open loop that makes stopping uncomfortable?
3. Is it specific (a number, a named thing, a vivid situation)?
4. Does it work muted (visual + text) *and* with sound?
5. Does the video fully pay it off?

## 7. Testing hooks

- **Same body, different hook.** Film 2–3 hook variants for strong scripts. On Instagram, run variants as Trial Reels (shown to non-followers first) or post the winning format later with a new hook.
- **Measure the right number:** 3-second hold / "viewed vs. swiped away" for the hook, and average watch % for the body.
- **Log every hook** with its type and result. After 20–30 posts, the audience's preferred hook types become obvious. Write more of those.
- **Recycle winners:** a hook type that worked can carry a different topic. A topic that worked can return with a new hook and format months later.
