## 1. Pre-production

- **Shot list from the script table.** Every row of the script has a visual. List each one as a shot with its framing, location and props. Group shots by location/setup so you film them together, not in script order.
- **B-roll list.** For each spoken beat, capture 2–3 supporting shots (hands, product, screen, environment, reaction). Get more than you think you need.
- **Batch filming.** Film 4–8 videos in one session: same setup, change shirt/background between videos. This is what makes posting consistently sustainable.
- **Wardrobe and set:** solid colors that fit the brand palette, no small patterns (they shimmer on camera), a background with depth (not flat against a wall), a few brand-relevant props, and no clutter.
- **Location sound check:** turn off fans and AC, close windows, avoid empty echoey rooms.

## 2. Gear tiers

Upgrade in this order, because each step gives the biggest quality jump per dollar:

| Tier | Kit | Cost (rough) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | A recent phone, a window, a stack of books as a tripod | $0 |
| 1 | **+ Lav or wireless mic** (biggest single upgrade) + phone tripod/clamp | $20–$250 |
| 2 | + One soft key light (LED panel with softbox, or ring light for talking heads) | $50–$200 |
| 3 | + Second light (fill/background), teleprompter app or rig, gimbal for movement | $150–$500 |
| 4 | Mirrorless camera, prime lens, audio interface, acoustic treatment | $1,000+ |

Most viral short-form is filmed at tier 0–2. Lo-fi phone content often outperforms polished ads in feeds because it looks native. Audio is the exception: **viewers forgive grainy video but not bad audio.**

## 3. Camera and framing

- **Use the rear camera** where possible (better sensor). With the front camera for self-filming, enable the highest resolution.
- **Resolution / frame rate:** 4K or 1080p at **30fps** for talking heads (24/25fps for a cinematic feel, 60fps for slow-motion B-roll). Keep frame rate consistent within a video.
- **Lock exposure and focus** (tap-and-hold on the face) so the brightness doesn't pump as you move.
- **Clean the lens.** Smudges are the #1 cause of hazy phone footage.
- **Framing for talking heads:** vertical 9:16, camera at eye level, eyes about a third from the top, small headroom, head-and-shoulders or chest-up. Leave room at the bottom for captions and UI.
- **Distance:** at least an arm's length. Wide phone lenses distort faces up close. Use 1x or 2x, not 0.5x, for faces.
- **Movement adds energy:** subtle push-ins, handheld walk-and-talk, or changing angles between sentences.

## 4. Lighting

- **Face the light source.** A window in front of you, or at about 45° to the side, gives soft flattering light. Never film with a window behind you.
- **Key light at 45°**, slightly above eye level, diffused (softbox or bounced). Ring lights give even, flat light, which is fine for talking heads.
- **Avoid overhead ceiling light alone.** It makes eye-socket shadows.
- **Separate subject from background:** a small light or lamp behind or to the side adds depth.
- **Match color temperatures:** don't mix warm bulbs with cool daylight. Pick one and turn the other off.
- **Golden-hour light** (an hour after sunrise or before sunset) is free and beautiful for outdoor B-roll.

## 5. Audio

- **Use a mic close to the mouth:** lav about 15cm (6in) below the chin, or a wireless clip-on kit (e.g., DJI Mic, Rode Wireless GO). Both record a backup on the transmitter.
- **Room:** soft furnishings absorb echo (sofa, curtains, rugs, a closet full of clothes). Avoid bare rooms and bathrooms.
- **Levels:** peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS while recording, never clipping. Record 5 seconds of room silence ("room tone") for editing gaps.
- **Monitor:** do a 10-second test and listen with headphones before recording all the takes.
- **Fix in post if needed:** noise reduction / AI voice enhancement tools can rescue mediocre audio, but not clipped or distant audio.

## 6. Performing on camera

- **Energy +20%.** The camera flattens energy, so what feels slightly too much in the room looks normal on screen.
- **Talk to one person**, a specific friend, not "everyone out there".
- **Record in chunks:** one or two sentences per take. Look at the lens, pause, then deliver. This makes jump cuts clean and lets you do retakes per line.
- **Smile, or reset your face, before and after each take** so there are clean cut points.
- **Teleprompter apps** are fine. Put the text near the lens and read with natural pauses.
- **Hook first, three ways:** film the hook 2–3 times with different energy/wording. It's the most important shot.
- **Use your hands and props:** showing beats telling.

## 7. A-roll, B-roll and shot types

- **A-roll** = main footage (you talking, the core demo). **B-roll** = supporting visuals laid over or between A-roll.
- For talking-head formats, aim for B-roll on **~30–40%** of the runtime. It hides jump cuts, illustrates points, and resets attention.
- **B-roll sources:** hands-on process, product close-ups and textures, screen recordings (zoomed and cropped to the relevant part), location/establishing shots, reactions, archive photos, simple motion graphics, AI-generated visuals (labelled when realistic).
- **Shot variety:** wide (context), medium (person), close-up (detail/emotion), extreme close-up (texture/satisfying), overhead (cooking, desk, crafts), POV (first-person), over-the-shoulder (screens).

## 8. Editing for retention

- **The first frame is the hook.** No fade-in, no logo, no black frame. The video starts mid-sentence or mid-action.
- **Cut the silence.** Remove breaths, "um"s, pauses and repeated starts (jump cuts). Leave a tiny breath where it aids comprehension.
- **Change something every ~2–4 seconds:** a punch-in zoom (about 110–120%), a cut to B-roll, a text pop-up, an angle change, a graphic, or an SFX. Retention drops when the screen goes static.
- **Pace follows emotion:** fast cuts for energy and lists. Hold the shot on reveals, punchlines and emotional moments.
- **Every claim gets a visual:** if you say "the dashboard", show the dashboard.
- **Payoff gets space:** hold the result or reveal for 1–2 seconds.
- **End clean:** either cut immediately after the CTA, or loop (the last frame and line connect to the first). No long outros.
- **Color:** consistent warm/cool look across videos as part of the brand. Correct skin tones first.
- **Common editors:** CapCut, Instagram Edits, VN, InShot (mobile); Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut (desktop); Descript (edit by transcript).

## 9. Captions and on-screen text

Many people watch muted, and on-screen text also tells platforms what the video is about. Burn captions into the video for Reels, TikTok and LinkedIn (styled captions also lift retention on Shorts).

**Caption style**
- **2–5 words per caption chunk**, 1–2 lines, centered.
- Large bold sans-serif with a strong outline or box for contrast on any background.
- Highlight one keyword per line in a brand accent color (optional, but it reads as "pro").
- Place it in the **lower-middle of the safe zone** (roughly 60–70% down the frame), above the platform's caption/UI area.
- Accuracy matters: fix names, jargon and brand terms. Bad auto-captions look careless.

**Text overlays** (hooks, labels, step numbers)
- Hook text: top-middle of the safe zone, 3–7 words, on screen from frame 1 for 2–3+ seconds.
- Allow ~3–4 words per second of reading time.
- Use the same fonts and colors every time as a brand signature.

## 10. Music and sound

- **Licensing depends on the account type.** Personal/creator accounts can use the in-app trending library. **Business accounts** are limited to commercial libraries (Meta Sound Collection on Instagram; the Commercial Music Library on TikTok, whose license is TikTok-only). Ads need properly licensed music. See `compliance.md`.
- **Under voice-over:** music should sit well below the voice (roughly -20 to -25 dB under dialogue), with ducking. If you can't hear every word on phone speakers, it's too loud.
- **Sound design:** subtle whooshes on transitions, pops on text, hits on reveals. Use it sparingly, because overuse is grating.
- **Original audio** (your voice, a catchphrase, a distinctive sound) can become a trend other people use, which is free reach.
- **Natural sound** (sizzle, pour, click, unboxing) carries process videos. Sometimes no music is the better choice.

## 11. Safe zones (1080×1920)

Platform UI (profile name, caption, buttons, nav bar) covers the edges of vertical video. Keep anything important inside the universal safe area:

```
┌────────────────────────────┐  0
│  top ~250px: status bar,   │
│  profile, "Following" tabs │
├────────────────────────┬───┤  ~250
│                        │ r │
│   SAFE AREA            │ i │
│   ~900 × ~1400         │ g │
│   hook text: upper     │ h │
│   captions: ~60–70%    │ t │
│   down                 │ ~ │
│                        │150│
├────────────────────────┴───┤  ~1470
│  bottom ~450px: caption,   │
│  audio, CTA bar, nav       │
└────────────────────────────┘  1920
```

Instagram Reels covers the most at the bottom, so if a layout works there it works everywhere.

## 12. Export settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Container / codec | MP4, H.264 (High profile), yuv420p |
| Resolution | 1080×1920 (9:16) |
| Frame rate | Same as the source; 30fps typical |
| Bitrate | ~10–20 Mbps for 1080p (platforms re-compress; give them a clean source) |
| Audio | AAC, 48kHz, 128–320kbps, loudness around **-14 LUFS** integrated, peaks ≤ -1 dBTP |
| Upload | Native, via the app or official scheduler. Enable "upload at highest quality" in Instagram settings. No other-app watermarks. |

## 13. Covers and thumbnails

- **Reels cover:** a branded template with 2–5 words of title in the centre (the grid crops to 3:4, and the feed may crop more). A consistent cover style makes the profile grid look intentional and helps visitors binge.
- **Shorts:** choose a frame where the subject is clear and expressive. Shorts surfaces show less of the thumbnail, but search and the channel page show more.
- **Carousel slide 1** is the cover, so design it like a thumbnail.

## 14. Using AI in production

Useful and low-risk uses (no disclosure needed on major platforms): brainstorming, script drafts, caption generation, noise removal and voice enhancement, auto-reframing, colour correction, translating/dubbing (disclose if voices are cloned), and generating simple graphics.

Higher-risk uses that need care: realistic AI people, voices or scenes. Label them with the platform's AI label (see `compliance.md`), never imitate real people without consent, and don't present AI footage as a real customer or a real event. If an image/video generation tool is connected, it's well suited to B-roll, product-in-scene shots, stylised visual hooks and backgrounds.

## 15. Helper scripts in this skill

```bash
# Estimate timing per beat; flags long hooks and dense text cards
python scripts/script_tools.py time content/my-script.md

# Make an .srt caption file from the script table (approximate timings)
python scripts/script_tools.py srt content/my-script.md --out captions.srt --max-words 4

# Reframe any video to 1080x1920, burn captions inside the safe zone, normalise audio to -14 LUFS
python scripts/vertical_export.py raw.mov final.mp4 --captions captions.srt
python scripts/vertical_export.py landscape.mp4 final.mp4 --mode blur      # landscape → vertical with blurred background
python scripts/vertical_export.py raw.mp4 final.mp4 --start 2.5 --end 34   # trim
python scripts/vertical_export.py raw.mp4 final.mp4 --dry-run              # print the ffmpeg command only
```

`vertical_export.py` needs `ffmpeg` with libass. Run `--help` for caption styling options (font, size, colors, vertical position).
