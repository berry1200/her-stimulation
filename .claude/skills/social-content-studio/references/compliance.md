# Compliance and Platform Rules

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. For regulated industries, big campaigns or anything unclear, tell the user to check with a lawyer or the platform's current policy pages. Rules change, so verify specifics when it matters.

## 1. Sponsored content and endorsements

**Rule of thumb:** if there's any *material connection* (payment, free product, discount, affiliate commission, employment, family/business relationship), disclose it clearly, early and in plain words.

- **US (FTC):** disclosures must be "clear and conspicuous", meaning hard to miss.
  - Use plain words: **"Ad"**, **"Sponsored"**, **"Paid partnership with [Brand]"**, "[Brand] gave me this for free". Vague terms like "sp", "spon", "collab", "thanks [brand]" or "ambassador" alone don't count.
  - Put it in the **first line(s) of the caption, before the "…more" cut**, not buried among hashtags.
  - In video, **also say it and/or show it on screen**, because many viewers never read captions.
  - Platform tools (Instagram "Paid partnership" label, TikTok "Content disclosure / Branded content" toggle) should be used **in addition to** your own disclosure, not instead of it.
  - Creators and brands can both be held responsible. Don't make claims the brand couldn't prove, and don't review products you haven't used.
- **UK (ASA/CMA):** put "Ad" or "#ad" up front. EU countries have similar rules, and some require "Werbung"/"Publicité" etc. in the local language.
- **Affiliate links:** disclose them too ("I earn a commission if you buy through my link").

When writing a sponsored script: put a disclosure line in the caption's first line, add on-screen "Ad" or "Paid partnership" text early in the video, and turn on the platform toggle in the posting checklist.

## 2. AI-generated content labels

Disclosure is required for **realistic** synthetic media: AI-generated or substantially AI-altered people, voices, places or events that a viewer could mistake for real.

- **TikTok:** realistic AI-generated or significantly edited content must carry the AI-generated content label (toggle in post settings). TikTok also auto-detects C2PA content credentials. [Reported] Unlabelled realistic AI content can be penalised (e.g., removed from monetisation or the For You page for a period).
- **YouTube:** check "Altered or synthetic content" when uploading realistic AI content. YouTube shows a label, which is more prominent for sensitive topics (news, elections, health, finance).
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook):** an "AI info" label is applied when AI is detected via metadata or self-disclosed. Disclose realistic AI content, and also note it in the caption where helpful.
- **Ads:** each platform's ad policies add requirements, and the FTC treats deceptive AI depictions (e.g., fake "customer" testimonials) as deceptive advertising.
- **EU AI Act transparency obligations** apply to deepfakes and certain AI content in the EU.

**No disclosure needed** for production assistance: AI-drafted scripts and captions, noise removal, colour correction, auto-captions, background removal, beauty filters, upscaling, or clearly fantastical/animated content.

**Never:** clone a real person's voice or likeness without written consent; present AI people as real customers; fake news events; use AI to imitate a celebrity endorsement.

## 3. Music and audio rights

- **Personal/creator accounts** can use the in-app music library within the platform, under the platform's licence.
- **Business accounts:**
  - Instagram/Facebook: limited to the **Meta Sound Collection** (commercially cleared). The full popular library isn't licensed for business use, even for organic posts.
  - TikTok: limited to the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**. **The CML licence covers TikTok only**, so a video re-posted to Instagram/YouTube with a CML track isn't covered.
- **Ads** need music licensed for advertising (royalty-free/stock libraries with commercial licences, or direct licences).
- **Cross-posting:** strip platform-library music from exported files and re-add the music natively on each platform, or use music you hold a licence for everywhere.
- **Consequences** of unlicensed use: muted audio, blocked videos, copyright strikes, and lawsuits against brands.
- **Safe defaults for brands:** an original voice-over, natural sound, licensed stock music, or your own composed audio.

## 4. Originality, reposting and credit

- Instagram doesn't recommend accounts that mostly repost others' content without meaningful changes, across Reels, photos and carousels. Watermarks from other apps are deprioritized. Cropping or speeding up doesn't make content original.
- To use others' content: get permission, transform it (commentary, analysis, reaction with substance, remix with your own footage), and credit clearly (tag + name). Native remix/stitch/duet features handle attribution automatically.
- **Customer UGC:** ask for permission in writing (a DM "yes" is the minimum; a release for ads) and credit the creator.
- Don't use copyrighted footage, logos or characters in commercial content without rights.

## 5. Claims (health, finance, results)

- **Substantiate objective claims** ("clinically proven", "doubles your sales", "#1 rated") with evidence you can produce.
- **Results/testimonials:** show what's typical, or clearly state that results vary *and* what typical results are. Don't cherry-pick outliers as if they were normal.
- **Health/wellness/supplements:** no claims to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease. Be careful with weight-loss before/afters (platform ad policies restrict them heavily).
- **Finance/investing/crypto:** no guaranteed returns; add risk language; local rules may require licensing for advice. Platforms restrict financial ads.
- **Comparative claims** about competitors must be true and fair.
- **Professional advice** (medical, legal, financial) given as entertainment/education should say so, and must not be misleading.

## 6. People on camera, privacy and minors

- Get consent (ideally a written release) from identifiable people, especially for ads and business accounts.
- Minors: get parental consent; avoid showing schools, locations or identifying details; follow platform rules for teen accounts; don't sexualise or exploit them.
- Don't film customers, patients or employees in sensitive situations without explicit consent. Blur screens showing private data (emails, addresses, client names).
- Public places: legal rules vary, but platform rules and common sense favour avoiding filming strangers in embarrassing situations.

## 7. Giveaways and contests

- Follow each platform's promotion rules: the platform must not be presented as endorsing it, plus official rules, eligibility, dates, how winners are chosen, and the prize value.
- Local laws matter (e.g., no purchase necessary rules in the US; registration requirements in some regions).
- "Tag 3 friends" entries can feel spammy. "Share to your story" or a "comment your answer" entry is usually better for brand perception.

## 8. Pre-publish compliance checklist

- [ ] Any material connection? → disclosure in the first caption line + on-screen/verbal + platform toggle
- [ ] Realistic AI people/voices/scenes? → AI label on, no deceptive use, consent for any likeness/voice
- [ ] Music licensed for this account type, platform and use (organic vs. ad)?
- [ ] Content original or properly licensed/credited; no third-party watermarks?
- [ ] Objective claims substantiated; results typical or qualified?
- [ ] Health/finance/regulated topic checked against platform policy?
- [ ] Consent from identifiable people (and parents for minors)?
- [ ] No private data visible on screens or documents?
